## Huygens’ Principle

**Huygens’ Principle** is a simple way to picture how waves move forward.

The idea is this: imagine a wavefront as a line or surface of points that are all moving together.

Each point on that wavefront acts like a tiny source and sends out a small circular or spherical wave. After a short time, all of those little waves overlap. The surface that just touches them all is the new wavefront.

This does not mean light is really made of tiny independent sources. It is a useful geometric picture to show how the shape of a wave can change as it travels.

### Key Terms

#### Point Source
A **point source** is an imagined point that sends waves out in every direction.

In Huygens’ view, every point on a wavefront behaves like a tiny point source.

#### Wavefront
A **wavefront** is a line or surface where the wave has the same phase.

For example, the crest of a wave is one wavefront. In a beam of light, a wavefront is the set of points that are all rising and falling together.

#### Wavelet
A **wavelet** is the small wave produced by each point on the original wavefront.

These wavelets spread forward and overlap with one another.

#### Envelope
The **envelope** is the shape that touches the outside of all the wavelets.

That envelope is the new wavefront after a short time.

## How a Lens Uses Wavefronts

A lens works by changing the shape of the wavefront that passes through it.

A flat wavefront travels straight. If a lens makes the wavefront curved, then the light rays are redirected so they meet at a focus.

- A **convex lens** slows light more in the center than at the edges. This makes the wavefront become curved inward, and the light converges to a point.
- A **concave lens** slows light more at the edges than in the center. This makes the wavefront bend outward, and the light diverges.

In other words, a lens does not just bend single rays. It changes the entire wavefront so that all rays arrive together at the desired place.

### Why the Shape Matters

The shape of the wavefront determines where light goes:

- A flat wavefront stays flat and travels straight.
- A curved wavefront can bend inward and focus.
- A curved wavefront can also bend outward and spread.

A standard glass lens does this by making light travel different distances through glass. A metalens does the same thing with tiny surface structures instead of bulk glass.

## A Simple Picture of Focusing

Imagine a flat wavefront approaching a lens.

If the lens delays the center of the wave more than the edges, the wavefront becomes like the surface of a bowl.

That bowl-shaped wavefront brings the light to a point. That point is the focus.

This is the basic reason a lens can make an image.

---

## Why This Matters for Metalenses

Metalenses use the same idea as ordinary lenses, but they do it on a flat surface.

Each tiny part of a metalens changes the phase of the passing light. Together, these changes shape the wavefront into the curve needed for focusing.

So the key idea is:

- A lens works by shaping wavefronts.
- Huygens’ Principle gives us a useful way to understand wavefronts.
- Metalenses are a modern way to control wavefronts using engineered surfaces.

That is how a lens focuses light: by transforming a wavefront so that all the light adds up at the right place.
