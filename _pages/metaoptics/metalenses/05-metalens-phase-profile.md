Metalens Phase Profile

A metalens applies the correct local phase shifts across its flat surface so incoming light converges to a focal point.

The design target is a hyperbolic phase profile:
$$\Phi(r) = -k \left( \sqrt{r^2 + f^2} - f \right),$$
where each radial position $r$ compensates for the extra path length to the focus.

For small angles near the optical axis, this phase profile reduces to the familiar quadratic lens phase, which is why metalenses mimic traditional lens behavior in the paraxial regime.

A metalens does not rely on glass thickness. It uses engineered meta-atoms to impose the required phase locally at each point on the surface.

**Key ideas**
- A metalens maps a plane wave to a converging spherical wave.
- The phase gradient controls ray direction toward focus.
- Local meta-atoms implement the required phase shift.

> Metalens focusing is driven by the spatial phase profile and its gradient, not by bulk lens thickness.
