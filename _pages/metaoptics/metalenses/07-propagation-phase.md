# Propagation Phase

Propagation phase is the phase accumulated by light as it travels through a subwavelength waveguide structure.

When light enters a dielectric nanopillar, it is confined and propagates as a guided mode rather than a free-space wave. The phase accumulated over the nanopillar's height is determined by the mode's propagation constant, which relates directly to its effective refractive index [1].

Because the effective refractive index depends on the pillar's cross-sectional geometry, designers can control the output phase by varying the diameter of individual meta-atoms [2]. This simple waveguide mechanism provides a reliable way to design transmission-type dielectric metalenses.

* Key mechanisms:
  * Modal confinement within the nanostructure.
  * Waveguide-mode propagation constant tuning.
  * Phase control via diameter adjustments.

> Takeaway: Propagation phase enables phase control by varying the effective refractive index through meta-atom geometry.
