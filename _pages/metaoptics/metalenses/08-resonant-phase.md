# Resonant Phase

Resonant phase is the phase modification that occurs when light interacts with the electromagnetic resonances of a subwavelength structure.

When incident light matches the resonant frequency of a meta-atom, the structure behaves like a truncated cavity, strongly trapping and scattering light [1]. The phase of the transmitted light changes rapidly near these resonances, allowing a full 2π phase shift range.

Dielectric meta-atoms utilize Mie-type and Fabry-Pérot resonances to control light [2]. By adjusting parameters like diameter and height, designers shift these resonance positions to engineer the desired optical phase profile.

* Key features:
  * Strong field localization inside the meta-atom.
  * Rapid phase variations near resonant frequencies.
  * Rely on Mie or Fabry-Pérot cavity modes.

> Takeaway: Resonant phase controls light by adjusting subwavelength cavity resonances to achieve rapid phase modulation.
